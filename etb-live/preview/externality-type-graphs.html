Reading externality graphs

The practice begins with what an externality means, then separates the two graph questions: where the spillover comes from and whether it helps or harms others. Students put those answers together before explaining the curve positions.

Review prototype · graph items and feedback only · no scored responses are recorded

Start with the meaning

Positive externality

Someone outside the transaction benefits. The market tends to do too little of the activity; society would gain from more.

Negative externality

Someone outside the transaction is harmed. The market tends to do too much of the activity; society would gain from less.

Positive · production

Example: research creates knowledge other firms can use.

Market: too little production. Curves: MSC (S2) below MPC (S1).

Positive · consumption

Example: vaccination reduces disease spread.

Market: too little consumption. Curves: MSB (D2) above MPB (D1).

Negative · production

Example: factory pollution harms nearby residents.

Market: too much production. Curves: MSC (S2) above MPC (S1).

Negative · consumption

Example: driving adds congestion for others.

Market: too much consumption. Curves: MSB (D2) below MPB (D1).

How the practice builds

  1. Identify the graph source: cost and supply curves point to production; benefit and demand curves point to consumption.
  2. Classify the spillover as positive or negative, using the idea of benefit or harm to third parties.
  3. Combine the two labels to name the externality type.
  4. Explain the evidence: compare MSC with MPC for production, or MSB with MPB for consumption. Connect that gap to too much or too little activity.

The graphs below are a final mixed set: three examples of each type, in scrambled order. Categories repeat, so each graph is classified on its own rather than matched one-to-one.

Mixed graph set

For each graph, identify its source (production or consumption) and sign (positive or negative). Then use the curve comparison to explain what the market does too much or too little of.

Each curve is labeled at its right-hand end in matching color, pairing D/S shorthand with the chapter term.

The graphs omit equilibrium quantities, prices, policy wedges, and welfare areas to keep attention on the externality type.

Show instructor key and curve rules
  • Production: S2 is MSC and S1 is MPC. MSC above MPC means negative; below means positive.
  • Consumption: D2 is MSB and D1 is MPB. MSB above MPB means positive; below means negative.
  • The unchanged market curve is D = MPB = MSB on production graphs, and S = MPC = MSC on consumption graphs.
