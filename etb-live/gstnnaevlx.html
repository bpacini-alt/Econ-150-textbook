Unit Intro

The Case for Intervention

The last fourteen chapters showed markets at their best. This unit puts pressure on the assumptions of the last fourteen chapters to see where cracks emerge. 

We begin my taking the pro-market case on its own terms: is it really a good thing for markets to maximize efficiency in the case of a repugnant market? What of dwarf-tossing? What of prostitution? Cigarettes? Do we really want to ensure that the maximum number of people are able to participate in such a market?

We then move to the underpinnings of markets: rule of law, property rights, political stability, infrastructure, a belief in the universal dignity of every human being, and much, much more. In short, there is no such thing as a free market. In other words, a free market is created; it is not merely the absence of government intervention.

Then, we dive into the rich economics of market failures: when markets do not create efficient outcomes on their own. These include third-party effects (known as spillovers, or sometimes externalities), information asymetry, market power, and goods that do not function like normal market goods. From here we turn to the rules and assumptions markets quietly stand on (ownership, exclusion, enforcement) and interrogate them one at a time. Finally, we turn to healthcare, where every one of these failures fires at once.

Sometimes markets work flawlessly and improve material conditions. At other times, markets produce suboptimal outcomes due to market failures. 

But perhaps the biggest critique of all is simply this: markets have no telos. There is no inherent end goal in a market except to maximize consumption. 

Is that a goal worth pursuing?

The thread

A thread runs through all of it: markets work fantastically well when they work, and they work much of the time; but when they don’t, there is a case for intervention of various kinds. This half of the book will not tell you how to address every problem, but it will give you a lens through which to better analyze whether a market is functioning.
