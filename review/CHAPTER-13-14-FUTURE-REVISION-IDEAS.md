# Future Revision Ideas — Chapters 13–14

**Status: Deferred.** This is an idea archive, not an implementation plan or commitment to revise Chapters 13–14 now.

The current Chapters 13–14 should remain as they are while larger priorities take precedence. The ideas below preserve useful work from Econ pull requests #40 and #55 without keeping those implementation branches active. Revisit them together in a future chapter revision.

## Editorial and accuracy ideas from PR #40

- Clarify the conditions and limits of the competitive-market explanation of discrimination.
- Replace the undated gender-pay statistic with a dated, sourced measure, and explain what that measure does and does not establish.
- Keep the benefits-cliff example, but label it clearly as an intentionally extreme illustration rather than a forecast; distinguish an effective marginal rate from a statutory tax rate.
- Calibrate references to the EITC, Saver’s Match, stablecoins, and current account-policy details against primary sources when the chapter is revised.
- Present the real-return assumption in the wealth figures as a constant illustration, not a prediction.
- Preserve the broader arc: take market explanations seriously, state their conditions and limits, and prepare students to encounter the next chapter’s counterarguments.

## Practice ideas from PR #55

These are possible practice activities, not a finalized recurring practice system:

- Retrieval and explanation questions tied to the central concepts.
- “Choose the tool” tasks in which students decide whether a graph, table, model, or other representation is useful.
- “Judge the use” tasks in which students evaluate whether a tool or explanation fits the question.
- Carefully bounded FRQ-style application and reasoning.
- Student choice among practice tasks, with optional test-out and room to skip material already mastered.
- A future practice layer should remain compatible with the broader exploratory Precise Practice design work; it should not become a mandatory chapter template by accident.

## Revisit conditions

When this work returns, review the chapter prose and practice together, recheck current policy references, compare the chapter’s role in the full Econ sequence, and build a fresh branch from the then-current main branch. Do not revive these old implementation branches as-is.
