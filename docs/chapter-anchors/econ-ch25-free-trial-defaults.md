# The Fine-Print Free Trial

- **Book / chapter:** *Joy of Economics*, Chapter 25, “Behavioral Economics.”
- **Anchor:** The Fine-Print Free Trial.
- **Core idea:** Choice architecture shapes behavior through salience and defaults. A prominent trial button draws attention while automatic-renewal terms and an unchecked email opt-out receive less notice.
- **Chapter source:** [`etb-live/Chapter25.html`](../../etb-live/Chapter25.html), reviewed on Econ main `a48e2f9`; the opening hook on defaults, “Major concept 2: Behavioral Biases,” and “The Same Nudge Can Help People—or Exploit Them.”
- **Scene mapping:** The large “START FREE TRIAL” button is visually dominant. The small auto-renewal disclosure has no checkbox; the separate, empty checkbox says “Select here to opt out of future emails.” Leaving it empty means the user has not opted out.
- **Misconception to avoid:** The point is not that consumers are foolish or that every default is harmful. Choice architecture can help or exploit, depending on who designs it and whose goals it serves.
- **Placement:** At the chapter start, after the title card and before the organ-donation opening hook.
