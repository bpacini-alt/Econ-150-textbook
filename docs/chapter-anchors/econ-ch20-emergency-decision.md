# The Pen in Midair

- **Book / chapter:** *Joy of Economics*, Chapter 20, “Unit Capstone: The Economics of Healthcare.”
- **Anchor:** The Pen in Midair.
- **Core idea:** Emergency patients must make consequential healthcare decisions under time pressure, with less information and expertise than the clinician and little opportunity to compare providers.
- **Chapter source:** [`etb-live/Chapter20.html`](../../etb-live/Chapter20.html), reviewed on Econ main `c733edf`; opening hook and “Major concept 1: Asymmetric Information.”
- **Scene mapping:** A patient's pen pauses over a blank consent form while a clinician points to a chest X-ray. The clock and visible ambulance establish urgency; the clinician's scan represents expertise the patient cannot readily evaluate. No price is shown because the chapter emphasizes that the patient does not face the full price at the point of emergency care.
- **Misconception to avoid:** The patient is not foolish and the clinician is not a villain. The decision is difficult because of urgency and unequal information, not because either person is acting in bad faith.
- **Placement:** At the chapter start, after the title card and before the opening hook.
