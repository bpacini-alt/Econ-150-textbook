## Workflow

1. Read the chapter's central explanation and nearby examples. Record the current source file, section heading, and the branch or commit reviewed.
2. Propose a short anchor name and a one-sentence statement of the chapter idea. Describe the scene, how its parts map to the idea, and one misconception it must avoid.
3. Record the proposal in the ledger and mark **Idea status: Proposed**. Keep it as proposed until Benjamin selects or revises the concept.
4. Generate an image in that book's established visual style. Keep it text-free by default; preserve specific labels or numbers Benjamin approves, including the Chapter 24 gas prices. Present the exact candidate for review.
5. Mark **Image status: Approved** only after Benjamin explicitly approves that exact image. Style feedback or approval of a different image does not count.
6. After approval, place the exact approved asset in the author-selected destination and record its filename, source note, and link. **Current author direction:** chapter images belong in the textbook repositories, with accessible chapter figures and merge PRs. Library folders remain an optional archive when that service is available; do not claim Library filing without verifying it.

## Status guide

**Idea status**

- **Not started:** No chapter-specific idea has been drafted.
- **In progress:** The idea is being developed.
- **Proposed:** The idea has been presented for review.
- **Selected:** Benjamin selected the concept; image approval is still separate.
- **No anchor recommended:** A deliberate exception, with the reason recorded.

**Image status**

- **Not started:** No candidate image has been generated.
- **Candidate shown:** A specific image is awaiting a decision.
- **Revision requested:** The image is being revised or replaced.
- **Approved:** Benjamin explicitly approved this exact image; the asset is recorded at the author-selected destination. Approval is separate from whether its PR has merged.
- **Not applicable:** The chapter has a documented “No anchor recommended” decision.

## Proposal record

When a chapter's idea status moves beyond **Not started**, put enough information in its ledger row to explain the choice. If the row becomes too long, add a linked detail entry below the ledger using this template:

- **Chapter / source:** Book, chapter number and title; repository path, section heading, and reviewed branch or commit.
- **Central idea:** One sentence.
- **Anchor name:** Short and memorable.
- **Scene and mapping:** What is shown and how each important part represents the idea.
- **Misconception to avoid:** One likely false inference the image must not suggest.
- **Idea status:** In progress, Proposed, Selected, or No anchor recommended.
- **Image status:** Not started, Candidate shown, Revision requested, Approved, or Not applicable.
- **Approved image:** Exact Library filename and link, only after explicit approval.
- **Reproduction notes:** Final prompt and book style notes, stored with the approved Library item.

## Premises proposed for review

These are the original November 20 concept proposals. Later placement decisions and research candidates are recorded in the decision log below.

### Joy of Economics — Batch 1 (three chapters)

- **Chapter 3 · One Price, Many Decisions:** A price carries local information through a simple chain of buyers and producers, letting each adjust without a central planner. **Guardrail:** Coordination does not mean every market outcome is fair or automatically ideal. **Source:** [Chapter 3](../etb-live/Chapter3.html), “What Prices Know,” Econ main `44ac046`.
- **Chapter 5 · The Two-Task Swap:** Two producers specialize where each gives up less, then trade so both can have more than if they produced alone. **Guardrail:** Comparative advantage is about opportunity cost, not who is best at everything. **Source:** [Chapter 5](../etb-live/Chapter5.html), “Specialization and Comparative Advantage” and “Gains from Trade,” Econ main `44ac046`.
- **Chapter 17 · The Deal’s Third Neighbor:** A buyer and seller make a deal while someone outside it bears a cost or receives a benefit the price leaves out. **Guardrail:** An externality is a spillover to someone outside the transaction, not simply any consequence. **Source:** [Chapter 17](../etb-live/Chapter17.html), “Externalities,” Econ main `44ac046`.

### Joy of Economics — Batch 2 (two chapters)

- **Chapter 18 · The Fish Left for the Next Boat:** Each fisher has reason to catch fish now because any fish left behind may be caught by someone else; open-access use drains the shared lake. **Guardrail:** The problem is the incentive structure, not selfish villains; users can create rules that change it. **Source:** [Chapter 18](../etb-live/Chapter18.html), “The Commons Trap” and “Ostrom’s Third Way,” Econ main `44ac046`.
- **Chapter 21 · The Only Bridge:** A seller controls the only practical route to a good, so buyers lack close substitutes and the seller can influence price by limiting output. **Guardrail:** A firm’s size alone does not make it a monopoly, and a monopolist still faces demand. **Source:** [Chapter 21](../etb-live/Chapter21.html), “Monopoly and Market Power,” Econ main `44ac046`.

### Joy of Learning — Batch 1 (three chapters)

- **Chapter 4 · Research Informs the Lesson:** A teacher rethinks a reading strategy when research shows a familiar method is misdirecting students, while still using professional judgment for the students in front of them. **Guardrail:** Evidence-based practice informs judgment; it is not mechanical rule-following. **Source:** [Chapter 4](https://github.com/bpacini-alt/edcor-310-textbook/blob/main/etb-live/chapter4.html), “Evidence-Based Practice” and “From Evidence to Action,” Learning main `56aabcc`.
- **Chapter 12 · Watch, Then Try:** A learner watches someone model a skill and then tries it, showing that learning can happen without direct reinforcement first. **Guardrail:** Keep this focused on observational learning; the chapter also treats self-efficacy, social contagion, and guided learning. **Source:** [Chapter 12](https://github.com/bpacini-alt/edcor-310-textbook/blob/main/etb-live/chapter12.html), “Observational Learning,” Learning main `56aabcc`.
- **Chapter 19 · The Attention Loop:** A teacher responds to one student while others watch, revealing how attention and consequences can shape behavior—including behavior the teacher hopes to reduce. **Guardrail:** Behaviorism is a useful account of observable learning, not the whole story of learning or motivation. **Source:** [Chapter 19](https://github.com/bpacini-alt/edcor-310-textbook/blob/main/etb-live/chapter19.html), “Behavior charts and trackers, explained” and “Operant Conditioning,” Learning main `56aabcc`.

### Joy of Learning — Batch 2 (two chapters)

- **Chapter 20 · The Unassigned Calculator:** A student spends a weekend building and debugging a calculator because the project matters to them, not because anyone assigned, graded, or rewarded it. **Guardrail:** Intrinsic motivation can include frustrating work; it is not just fun or ease. **Source:** [Chapter 20](https://github.com/bpacini-alt/edcor-310-textbook/blob/main/etb-live/chapter20.html), “Intrinsic Motivation” and “Supporting Student Motivation,” Learning main `56aabcc`.
- **Chapter 22 · The Four-Slot Workbench:** A student’s active mental workspace has room for only a few pieces at a time, so clutter and competing stimuli can crowd the lesson out. **Guardrail:** Treat the “four” as an approximate working-memory finding, not a fixed score or a measure of a student’s overall ability. **Source:** [Chapter 22](https://github.com/bpacini-alt/edcor-310-textbook/blob/main/etb-live/chapter22.html), “Selective Attention,” “Memory,” and “Cognitive Load Theory,” Learning main `56aabcc`.

### Learning Chapter 24: research shortlist — curb cut selected

These are researched possibilities for new chapter content. Benjamin subsequently selected item 2, the curb-cut story. Its concept fits existing sections and still needs a short sourced vignette; items 1 and 3 remain unselected alternatives.

1. **The explanation that became unnecessary — expertise reversal.** Kalyuga, Chandler, and Sweller (1998) found across three experiments that the best diagram/text design changed with expertise: integrated explanations helped less-expert learners, while eliminating redundant text helped more-expert learners. Fits the Instructional Hierarchy/readiness discussion. A circuit-training scene would be an illustration of the finding, not a claim to reproduce the experimental setup. **Guardrail:** Adjust support to demonstrated knowledge; do not assign permanent learner types. The broader novice/expert principle overlaps Chapter 2 and cognitive-load theory in Chapter 22, although this specific study is not named there. [Primary study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1518/001872098779480587).
2. **The midnight curb ramp — access designed by the people who need it.** Berkeley disability activists used asphalt to create unauthorized curb ramps while official installation lagged. Strong historical-story candidate for Universal Design for Learning and removing barriers. Possible scene: a wheelchair user directing or trying a new ramp as a fellow activist finishes its edge under a streetlight. **Guardrail:** Distinguish the documented asphalt work from dramatic sledgehammer/nitroglycerin lore; universal access does not eliminate individualized supports. [UC Berkeley archival history](https://revolution.berkeley.edu/projects/place-every-body/).
3. **The concert-ticket algebra problem — interest as a useful difference.** Walkington and Sherman's 2012 primary report describes a randomized study of 145 Algebra I students: changing story contexts to match students' interests supported performance, including writing algebraic expressions. Possible scene: a student working out concert-ticket revenue with the same underlying algebra as a conventional construction-cost problem. Fits “Interest as the Most Actionable Variable.” **Guardrail:** Context personalization is not learning-style matching; the study does not isolate interest from topic familiarity. This has thematic overlap with Chapter 20's motivation discussion. [Author-hosted primary report](https://www.smu.edu/-/media/site/simmons/research/rme/docs/cw/icls_submission_2012_final.pdf).

### Econ Chapters 21–22: Standard Oil and the Bell System
- Benjamin explicitly approved the Standard Oil and Bell System illustrations. Both are placed after the chapter title card and before the opening hook, with responsive image sizing, descriptive alt text, and concept captions.
- **Chapter 21:** The refinery and tank-car network make Standard Oil a concrete example of durable monopoly power. The picture is illustrative, not a literal map or a claim that Standard Oil owned every oil producer. Source: etb-live/Chapter21.html, “Monopoly and Market Power” and “The Inefficiency of Monopoly.”
- **Chapter 22:** The Bell System is labeled by its historical name. “Ma Bell” was a popular nickname for the integrated system associated with AT&T and the regional Bell operating companies. AT&T divested its local operating companies in 1984 and continued its long-distance business; in 2005, former regional Bell company SBC acquired AT&T Corp. and adopted the AT&T name. Sources: [AT&T brand history](https://about.att.com/innovation/intellectualproperty/ip_brands/ip_history.html) and the [Federal Judicial Center’s history of the breakup](https://www.fjc.gov/history/spotlight-judicial-history/breakup-ma-bell). Source chapter: etb-live/Chapter22.html, “Natural Monopoly and the Regulator’s Dilemma.”
- **Reviewed ref:** Econ main 35670873.
