## Decisions the next chat must preserve

- **Baseball belongs in Learning 11, Constructivism.** It already illustrates the prior-knowledge study. Keep it there; Learning 24 uses the curb-cut story. The baseball result is not evidence for a kinesthetic learning style.
- **Econ 1:** Benjamin selected Bastiat's broken window and forgone suit over the giant-nail parable. The unbought suit is essential to the opportunity-cost mapping.
- **Econ 2:** the diamond–water storefront scene won over hot-dog and kidney-exchange drafts. Preserve the approved smaller foreground bottle and the illustrative $1 / $5,000 price cards.
- **Learning study guide:** the almost blank retrieval attempt must remain visible. Highlighting alone can create familiarity; the caption points toward explanation and retrieval, without implying the student cannot learn.
- **Learning 2:** use the approved fable version, not the earlier contemporary tourist outfit. The rider points left while the calm elephant walks right. Preserve both direction and scale; intuition is not an enemy to suppress.
- **Learning 20:** keep the unsolved candle problem in the opening so readers can try it before seeing the solution. The solution image stays with the answer.
- **Learning 24:** the wheelchair user has a relaxed smile and thumbs-up. An earlier expression appeared angry at the fellow activist and was revised.
- **Econ 23:** the cafés are differentiated through typography, muted color, and atmosphere while selling the same broad product. **Learning 23:** first-person desk view in aligned exam rows, a proctor with desk/chalkboard/clock, quieter windows, and varied recruits.
- **Econ intro:** the opening pass retained the existing Korea-at-night photograph; it was not a request to generate a replacement.
