| **1. Thinking like an economist** | Opportunity cost, incentives, seen/unseen effects, positive/normative; *good intentions abolish trade-offs*. | “The unseen cost is still a cost”; **Sowell, VERIFY** “There are no solutions, only trade-offs”; “Every yes is a no to something else”; **EXISTING heading** “Empiricism: reality gets the final vote”; Bastiat's seen-and-unseen framework. |

| **4. Shifts, shocks, price controls** | Curve shifts versus movements; price adjustment and rationing; *a ceiling eliminates scarcity*. | **AUTHOR-PROPOSED** “You can ration with prices, lines, or luck, but you will still ration”; **STUDY GUIDE** “Rationing never disappears; only its currency changes”; “When prices can't move, scarcity finds another way”; “If the price can't rise, the line might”; “Controlling the price doesn't control the scarcity.” |
| **5. Trade and specialization** | Comparative advantage is lower opportunity cost; *absolute best producer should do everything*. | “Your advantage isn't what you do best. | **6. Welfare** | Total surplus measures gains from trade; efficiency and equity are distinct; *the pie is fixed or equality is what efficiency measures*. | “Deadweight loss is a win-win that never happened”; “Some of the costliest losses are trades that never occur”; “Efficiency asks about the pie. | **7. Taxes and subsidies** | Elasticity determines actual burden and who captures subsidy; *statutory taxpayer bears cost*. | “The side with fewer exits bears more of the tax”; “The law names the taxpayer. | **8. International trade** | Gains, adjustment costs, tariffs and distribution; *imports always impoverish us*. | “Trade turns somebody else's advantage into yours”; “Trade is a technology for turning what we make into what we want”; “A tariff doesn't bring jobs home for free”; “Trade can grow the pie without giving everyone a bigger slice”; *luggage* candidate as possible echo from Econ 14. |

## EDCOR 310 — Unit I

| Chapter | Thick concept / tempting mistake | Lines to compare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Purpose** | Purpose, becoming and rigorous thought; *rigor equals suffering or workload*. | “The difficulty is not what you get through. It is what makes you”; “Rigor is about thinking, not suffering”; “A difficult assignment isn't necessarily a demanding one”; “The goal of education isn't the task. It's the person the task helps form”; Bjork desirable-difficulties distinction, VERIFY. |
| **2. Biases** | Thinking is systematically fallible; education cultivates intuitions and habits, not facts alone; *knowing biases makes you immune*. | “The rider learns the reasons. | **3. Empiricism** | Evidence, falsifiability, model simplification and limits of purely factual claims; *imperfect assumptions invalidate the scientific endeavor*. | **EXISTING subtitle, opening story, and synthesis** “The Bomb Worked!” (Dr. Dibble anecdote); “A theory that cannot lose cannot learn”; **Box, VERIFY** “All models are wrong, but some are useful”; “The experiment gets a vote your intuition doesn't”; “The best theory survives contact with reality.” |
| **4. Evidence-based practice** | Study design and evidentiary standards, professional judgment and values; *an inspiring case study or sincere intention proves efficacy*. | “Evidence guides judgment; it cannot replace it”; “Good intentions cannot substitute for good evidence”; “A good teacher can be sincerely wrong”; “An inspiring story isn't necessarily a fair test.” |
| **5. Statistical significance** | Error, null hypothesis, p values and replication; *significant = certainly true*. | “A statistically significant result can still be wrong”; “Significant doesn't mean certain”; **Cohen, VERIFY** “The earth is round (p < .05)”; “A verdict is only as trustworthy as the trial that produced it.” |
| **6. Practical significance** | Effect sizes, costs and magnitude, personal and practical stakes; *measurable = important*. | “Real is not the same as important”; “Statistically significant isn't necessarily significant”; “Don't just ask whether it works. Ask how much, for whom, and at what cost”; “A tiny effect can make a big headline.” |
| **7. Mental health** | Evidence without false certainty; compassion plus coping, avoid inappropriate overprotection; *good support removes all discomfort*. | “Preserve the goal; redesign the path”; “Support doesn't always mean removing the challenge”; “Help students face manageable difficulty, not face it alone”; “Relief today can strengthen avoidance tomorrow” (context-dependent; needs caution). |
| **8. Positive psychology** | Flourishing exceeds symptom absence, relational well-being and challenges; *kindness and standards conflict*. | “Warmth and challenge are two dials, not one slider”; “Resilience isn't being left alone to suffer”; “The absence of misery isn't the fullness of happiness”; “Help people flourish, not merely cope.” |
