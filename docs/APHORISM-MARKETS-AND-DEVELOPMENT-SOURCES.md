## I. Economic arguments in productive conversation

A strong textbook can set these perspectives against one another rather than presenting one team as villains and the other as heroes. **Quote status:** “Verified short excerpt” means sourced from an author's own published words, interview or a high-quality book excerpt. “Supported idea” is a paraphrase that **must not be printed as a quotation**. ### The deliberate debates — more useful than disconnected market slogans

1. **RESULTS vs INTENTIONS:** Friedman on judging consequences, with Stiglitz's analysis of market failures and Reich's examination of the rules producing those consequences. *Which actual market or policy arrangement does better under plausible conditions?*
2. **TRADE-OFFS vs INSTITUTIONAL REFORM:** Sowell's trade-off constraint, and Acemoglu/Robinson on institutions that can *change* the available choices and long-run economic opportunity. No magic fixes; important reforms can change the feasible frontier.
3. **SPONTANEOUS COORDINATION vs WHO SETS THE RULES?:** Hayek/Smith/Friedman on decentralized incentives, Acemoglu/Reich on power and legal structure, Stiglitz on limits of market efficiency. “The market” is not a single institutional arrangement.
4. **WHAT SCHOOL DOES:** Caplan's signaling hypothesis versus skill-building/human-capital mechanisms and students' broader purposes. A credential can both certify learning and provide labor-market information; empirical question is how much of each.
5. **INCENTIVES vs VIRTUE:** Levitt/Dubner show hidden incentives, but actual cooperation also relies on norms, information and enforceable rules. Source-first treatment fits *Freakonomics*' own bagel-trust example and Smith's moral psychology.

### Where these improve the economic heat map

- **Econ 1:** Sowell's scarcity and trade-offs; Friedman's outcomes-over-intentions. Both authentic sourced sentences outperform more generated slogans.
- **Econ 5 / 8:** Need more primary comparative-advantage source work (Ricardo, Krugman, modern trade). *Freakonomics* is not a substitute for comparative advantage.
- **Econ 14:** Acemoglu, Caplan, Lucas/poverty and geographic opportunity supply a richer poverty argument while respecting distribution and real institutional obstacles.
- **Econ 16–17:** Acemoglu and Reich supply the inclusive-rules/power question; pair with North and Ostrom from main quarry. Benjamin said **NO** to “An objection is not a veto” as an aphorism; do not resubmit it.
- **Econ 18–20:** Friedman (actual results), Sowell (trade-offs), Stiglitz (real market failures), and corrective-policy design are a coherent intellectual sequence.
- **Econ 21–22:** Distinguish competition as conduct from concentration as size; power and rent-seeking can reflect rules. Cross-check claims against firm entry/barriers evidence.
- **Econ 26:** Smith's moral character, Reich's rules, Stiglitz's limits, Friedman's liberty and Lucas's growth stakes can frame the chapter's closing conversation. Benjamin's own morality line remains **REWRITE**, not YES.

## II. Developmental psychology — original theories and memorable words

**Do not confuse Erik Erikson with “Erickson”**; the developmental psychologist is **Erik H. Erikson**. The ZPD psychologist is **Lev S. Vygotsky**. Avoid treating stage theories as rigid, deterministic timetables. An attractive quote can illuminate a theory without independently proving it.

| **Lev Vygotsky** | **“what a child can do with assistance today she will be able to do by herself tomorrow”** | **EDCOR 10/12** — development is more than what students can do alone *today*. Guided practice and social learning can support new independent capability. **Potential top candidate** for an educator-facing aphorism. | **Short excerpt from *Mind in Society* (1978), p. 87**; [academic journal article reproducing and paging original](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0742051X23000999). ### Development-specific opportunities that a generic aphorism list missed

- **EDCOR 10 / 12 (Vygotsky):** A beautifully teachable contrast is **what a student can do alone versus with help**. This is the mechanism of instructional scaffolding and can motivate calibrated challenge. Note that **“scaffolding” was named later** by Wood, Bruner and Ross (1976); avoid crediting that exact term to Vygotsky.
- **EDCOR 13 / 14 (Erikson):** Identity as a *social and developmental task*, and a “crisis” as a turning point, are more instructive than generic appeals to “be yourself.” Student ages and pathways vary.
- **EDCOR 10 (Piaget vs Bruner vs Vygotsky):** This could become a genuinely educational contrast: stage readiness, intellectually honest early representation/spiral curriculum, and guided capabilities in the zone of proximal development. Teach each theorist fairly; don't collapse them into a false three-way dispute or declare any a universal teaching recipe.
- **EDCOR 16 (Bandura):** Confidence needs something to be confident *about*; mastery is especially relevant, but modeling and persuasion also matter. The user has explicitly marked earlier phrasing **REWRITE**, not YES.
- **EDCOR 26 (Erikson and Palmer):** Adult generativity, integrity and teacher character create a strong source-led closing chapter on what educators pass on to students. Distinguish professional vocation from deterministic predictions about children.

## IV. Sourcing, interpretation and quote hygiene

- A short attributed passage may be directly quoted **only** if its wording is checked; book titles, paraphrases and editor-created compressions need their own labels.
- Political economy has real live empirical and normative disagreements. Present the mechanism and relevant limitations of each view: markets coordinate and fail; governments enable exchange and can also create capture; laws shape outcomes and face trade-offs. No false consensus and no reflexive equal-weighting of weak and strong evidence.
- For developmental psychology, separate theorists' **historically influential frameworks** from claims validated by current evidence. Erikson's precise stage structure and Kohlberg's moral reasoning phases remain contested; Vygotsky's ZPD is a powerful instructional lens, not a guarantee of learning; Bandura's encouragement is one *real* source of self-efficacy.
- Compare each idea to current chapter text and memorization quizzes before proposing insertion. At most two strong aphorism options per concept; no chapter quote quota. Preserve exact user ratings in merged PR #59, currently unreviewed Batch 8, and all source-only documentation.
