## Two user-recalled passages: confirmed and corrected

1. **Robert E. Lucas, Jr., “On the Mechanics of Economic Development” (1988), pp. 4–5:** “Once one starts to think about them, it is hard to think about anything else.” *Them* refers to the welfare consequences and policy questions implied by huge differences in countries' economic growth and income, **not numbers in general**. This is the economist **Robert Lucas**, not the magazine *The Economist*. Strong candidates: **ECON 14 (poverty and opportunity)**; **ECON 26** (human flourishing and why development matters). [Text excerpt with page reference](https://stafforini.com/works/lucas-1988-mechanics-economic-development/) · [Cambridge University Press secondary corroboration](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/economic-development-and-transition/development-transition-and-divergence/D381BB03DE61CB283CBF3CF0ECDF7FF7).
2. **George E. P. Box:** “Essentially, all models are wrong, but some are useful.” **Not** “All models are lies.” This concerns models as *approximations*, not deception. Box's shorter claim about models being wrong appears in 1976; the full aphorism is documented by 1978 (conference proceedings), republished in 1979, and appears in Box & Draper (1987), p. 424. Best for **ECON 1** (ceteris paribus, useful simplification) and **EDCOR 3** (empiricism). It could be a source-led companion to **“The Bomb Worked!”**, not a replacement. [SAS research note tracking provenance, with page images](https://blogs.sas.com/content/iml/2025/04/02/all-models-are-wrong.html) · [CAUSEweb statistical-education reference](https://causeweb.org/cause/resources/library/r1215).

## Where this source search could change the heat map

The *most* promising opportunities are not the already-strong price-system chapters. They are conceptual gaps where an authoritative, memorable original could outperform our generated candidates.

1. **ECON 1 — Box + Bastiat:** model humility, opportunity cost, and the distinction between seen and unseen consequences; original sources may be stronger than generic sacrifice phrasing.
2. **ECON 14 / 26 — Lucas + Krugman + Smith:** the scale of growth, productivity, human welfare and the moral ambitions and limitations of markets. This is the biggest conceptual upgrade over the current pie language.
3. **ECON 16 / 17 — North + Ostrom:** *rules and institutions* correct the oversimplified rule-free-market-versus-central-control binary. Especially valuable because Benjamin rejected “An objection is not a veto” as an aphorism.
4. **ECON 9 / 12 / 22 — Coase:** transaction costs explain why firms exist and when organization beats exchange. This may unlock the unfinished cost discussion.
5. **EDCOR 11 / 22 / 24 — Willingham + *Make It Stick* + Bjork + Pashler:** factual knowledge, mental models, retrieval, performance-versus-learning, and why rigid learning-style matching is misleading.
6. **EDCOR 3 / 5 / 6 — Box and his statistical perspective:** science needs useful models and honest uncertainty; not every statistically detected effect is educationally important.

## A REQUIRED source-first protocol before generating an original chapter aphorism

1. **Identify the thick concept.** Summarize the real mechanism, misconception, key boundary conditions, and what would change a student's mind.
2. **Read the current chapter in full for existing gems.** Check hook, story, cited thinker, major-concept headings, callouts, recap, and memorization quizzes. Existing language might already be the winner.
3. **Mine at least two fitting sources, not a general quote website.** Pick from the relevant original scholars/books above plus chapter-specific authors. Prefer actual books, original essays, author/publisher excerpts, and careful academic reproductions. Search for further sources where needed. Do not scrape or copy whole copyrighted chapters.
4. **Record what *exactly* the source says.** Separate **exact short quote / sourced paraphrase / original Benjamin or assistant wording**. A rough remembered variant isn't an attributed quote. Store stable source URL and page/edition if known; mark source checks outstanding.
5. **Only then propose up to two candidate anchors**, with one short unpacking, caveat, and novel application each. If no line is worth adopting, **recommend no new aphorism** for the moment.
6. **Benjamin rates YES / MAYBE / REWRITE / NO or leaves Not rated.** Do not infer approvals from enthusiasm or promotion requests. Rejected aphorism does not imply deletion of a published section heading. Integrate *only* by a separate reviewable textbook PR after affirmative placement approval.

**Copyright and ethics:** This quarry holds minimal identifying passages only; quote source words sparingly in student materials, attribute meaningfully, and never paste long copyrighted excerpts. **No textbook or memorization quiz was modified to create this research document.**
