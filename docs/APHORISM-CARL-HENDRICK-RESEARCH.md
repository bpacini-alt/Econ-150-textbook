## Why Hendrick is useful

Carl Hendrick's collaboration with Paul A. Kirschner and Jim Heal focuses on a central distinction for educational psychology: **what makes teaching look effective is not necessarily what makes learning durable or transferable**. His works synthesize original research; when attributing foundational principles, identify the original scientist rather than automatically assigning authorship to Hendrick.

Books to consult:

- *How Learning Happens* (Kirschner and Hendrick): seminal research on prior knowledge, memory, cognitive load, worked examples and transfer. [Publisher](https://www.routledge.com/How-Learning-Happens-Seminal-Works-in-Educational-Psychology-and-What-They-Mean-in-Practice/AKirschner-Hendrick/p/book/9781032498409).
- *How Teaching Happens* (Kirschner, Hendrick and Heal): landmark teaching and teacher-effectiveness research. [Publisher](https://www.routledge.com/How-Teaching-Happens-Seminal-Works-in-Teaching-and-Teacher-Effectivene/Heal-Hendrick-Kirschner/p/book/9781032132075).
- *Instructional Illusions* (Hendrick, Kirschner and Heal): ten illusions concerning engagement, expertise, student-centred learning, transfer, easy wins, motivation, discovery, uniqueness, performance and innovation. [Author introduction](https://carlhendrick.substack.com/p/introducing-instructional-illusions).
- *What Does This Look Like in the Classroom?* (Hendrick and Robin Macpherson): implications for explicit explanation, feedback, classroom culture and evidence-based routines. [Chartered College review](https://my.chartered.college/research-hub/why-you-should-read-what-does-this-look-like-in-the-classroom-by-carl-hendrick-and-robin-macpherson/).

## Research-to-practice implications

1. **Recall versus application** (EDCOR 22 and our precise-practice engine): build spaced retrieval, but also require students to select the right principle, explain why it applies, and solve new examples. One study discussed by Hendrick concerns improved spelling-rule recall without matching transfer to spelling application; do not generalize this to "retrieval doesn't work."
2. **Performance and engagement versus learning** (EDCOR 4/25): immediate fluency, entertaining activities and active-looking classrooms can coexist with weak retention. Preserve a measure of delayed understanding and transfer.
3. **Motivation versus capability** (EDCOR 16): empty praise need not create skills or self-efficacy; mastery experiences help. Preserve Benjamin's exact exploratory rewrite: **“evidence is more effective than empty praise” (REWRITE, not YES)**.
4. **Novice expertise versus guided instruction** (EDCOR 11): construction of knowledge is a description of cognition, not a rule that students should always discover everything unguided.
5. **Learning styles** (EDCOR 24): task-relevant visuals and good Tier 1 teaching can benefit many students without reducing students to types.
6. **Bloom's taxonomy** (EDCOR 4/25): separate classification of objectives from unsupported claims that teaching should always go from a pyramid's base to its apex.
7. **Curriculum and practice** (all chapters): build meaningful long-range connections and return paths rather than uncoordinated practice widgets.
8. **AI-dependent performance** (practice): fluency achieved with an AI giving answers should not be confused with student independence; use the AI for feedback and eliciting reasoning instead.
