# Catch the Politician: Engine and Content Plan

**Status:** Exploratory design note. This is not yet a live textbook feature.

## Purpose

“Catch the Politician” is a possible Hot Text activity for helping students identify when a persuasive policy argument outruns the economic reasoning that supports it.

The activity should teach students to catch reasoning—not ideology. A policy goal can be legitimate, and a student can support or oppose a policy, without that preference determining whether a sentence contains an economic error.

## Reusable interaction engine

The prototype establishes a promising interaction pattern:

- Students read a complete composite speech.
- They select every sentence that deserves a catch.
- They commit their selections together rather than receiving constant interruption.
- Feedback identifies the reasoning problem and explains why it matters.
- Students can retry, move among speeches, and see progress.
- Sentence controls remain inline, keyboard-accessible, and screen-reader friendly.
- The activity has no timer, partisan answer key, or grade by default.

Potential reasoning catches include:

- a possible mechanism presented as guaranteed;
- a conditional result treated as universal;
- a policy goal confused with the policy’s actual effect;
- a visible benefit presented without who pays or who loses;
- an opportunity cost or relevant tradeoff erased;
- a small estimated effect treated as zero;
- an uncertain forecast treated as money or output already secured.

## Content backlog

The first exploratory set considers:

1. price controls;
2. tax cuts and revenue;
3. minimum-wage policy;
4. tariffs and trade;
5. housing supply;
6. public spending and growth.

These are topic slots, not finished instructional examples. Each speech needs substantive development and review before it belongs in the live textbook.

## Requirements for a future content set

Before merging examples, review each set for:

1. **A plausible argument.** The speech should begin with a recognizable concern, value, or defensible premise.
2. **A precise catch.** The target sentence should contain a specific reasoning failure, not merely a controversial position.
3. **Useful feedback.** Students should learn what was wrong and what a better economic question would be.
4. **Clear normative/empirical separation.** A value judgment should not be marked wrong merely because it is a value judgment.
5. **Economic accuracy.** Claims, mechanisms, qualifications, and examples need source and content review.
6. **Fairness and neutrality.** The activity should not depend on identifying a partisan side or treating one policy family as inherently suspect.
7. **Preview testing.** Check wrapping, sentence selection, feedback, keyboard operation, mobile layout, and screen-reader labels.

## Recommended sequence

1. Preserve the prototype as a design reference.
2. Develop and review one or two examples rather than trying to finish all six.
3. Separate the reusable engine from the reviewed example content.
4. Test the activity with students or instructors.
5. Expand the content set only if the interaction produces useful reasoning practice.
