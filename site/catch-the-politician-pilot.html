Catch the Politician

A politician wants to make a persuasive case for a policy. The speech may contain real concerns and a defensible goal—but it may also move too quickly from a true statement to a misleading conclusion. Read the whole speech and select every sentence that deserves a catch.

The rule: Catch reasoning, not ideology. A policy can be worth debating even when a speech makes a bad argument for it. A value judgment is not automatically an error; the catch is when the speech treats that value as proof of an economic effect, hides who pays, or turns a conditional result into a guarantee.
  • Look for a possible mechanism presented as certain.
  • Look for a model or finding treated as universal.
  • Look for a cost, tradeoff, opportunity cost, or affected group that disappears.
  • Look for a policy goal being confused with the policy's actual effect.
